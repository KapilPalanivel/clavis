How to find forgotten Aadhaar, PAN and bank scans on your Windows PC

You probably know where your "KYC" folder is. The copies that worry people are the other ones: the e-Aadhaar PDF downloaded for a SIM card two years ago, a PAN photo a relative sent on WhatsApp, a bank statement saved for a loan application, a Form 16 from an old employer's email. They pile up in Downloads, WhatsApp Desktop, the Desktop and Pictures, often with names like IMG_2041.jpg or document(3).pdf. This guide shows where they hide on a Windows PC, how to find them, and how to lock them so a lost laptop or a repair shop can't read them.

Short answer: check Downloads, Desktop, Pictures, Documents and the hidden WhatsApp Desktop folder (%LOCALAPPDATA%\Packages\5319275A.WhatsAppDesktop_cv1g1gvanyjgm\LocalState\shared\transfers). File Explorer search finds copies with obvious names like "aadhaar" or "pan". To catch scans with meaningless names, the free Find sensitive files in Clavis Encrypt reads the text inside documents, pictures and PDFs on your PC, lists likely Aadhaar, PAN, passport, bank and tax documents with the reason for each, and encrypts the ones you tick. You review the list first, and nothing leaves your PC.

Step 1: search by name in File Explorer (5 minutes)

This catches files whose names give them away. Open File Explorer, go to your user folder (C:\Users\<you>), click the search box and try:

  • aadhaar OR aadhar OR adhar OR uidai (people spell it many ways; OR must be in capitals)
  • pan OR epan, then passport, voter, licence OR license
  • statement OR passbook, salary OR payslip, form16 OR "form 16" OR itr
  • Add ext:.pdf or ext:.jpg to narrow a long list.

For WhatsApp Desktop, paste %LOCALAPPDATA%\Packages\5319275A.WhatsAppDesktop_cv1g1gvanyjgm\LocalState\shared\transfers into File Explorer's address bar and press Enter. Name search has a clear limit: it won't tell you that IMG_2041.jpg is a photo of your PAN card. That's what step 2 is for.

Step 2: let Clavis look inside the files

Install Clavis Encrypt (free; Windows 10 and 11, also on the Microsoft Store), create a vault with a strong password, and keep the one-time recovery key safe. Then:

  1. Choose Find Sensitive Files in the sidebar.
  2. Under Where to look, keep Documents, Downloads, Desktop and Pictures, and WhatsApp ("Files received in WhatsApp on this PC") if it's listed. Press Add a folder… for anything else, such as D:\KYC or an old backup folder.
  3. Under What to read, leave Read text inside pictures and PDFs on. Clavis uses the text recognition built into Windows to read photos and the first page of PDFs, so a scanned PAN called scan0001.pdf can still be spotted. If you keep phone photos in Pictures, also tick Also read every photo in Pictures (slower with a big photo library).
  4. Press Start search. Files appear as they're found; you can switch to another page meanwhile, and Stop keeps what was found so far.
  5. Check the list. Likely sensitive files are ticked; Possibly sensitive ones (such as medical reports and cheques) are not ticked, so look at those yourself. Each file says why it's listed, for example Contains a PAN, Contains an Aadhaar number or File name says "passport". Use Show in folder or Open from the ⋯ menu if you're not sure.
  6. For a file you never want suggested again, choose ⋯ → Never suggest this file (you can Undo).
  7. Press Encrypt selected and confirm. Each file becomes a .enc file that opens only with your vault password, and the original is overwritten, then deleted.

Step 3: keep, or remove?

  • Copies you still need (your own Aadhaar, PAN, passport): keep them encrypted. When a form asks for a copy, double-click the .enc file, upload it, and close it. More on this in is it safe to keep Aadhaar and PAN scans on your laptop?
  • Duplicates and copies you don't need (five versions of the same e-Aadhaar, a relative's documents sent for one form): delete them. Clavis's free Shred overwrites a file before deleting it; that's best effort on SSDs, pen drives and memory cards, and backups or cloud version history may still hold a copy.
  • Where the full number isn't needed, use a masked Aadhaar (only the last four digits visible) from myaadhaar.uidai.gov.in. It still shows your name, photo and address, which is why Clavis lists masked copies too.

Step 4: clean up the copies that aren't on your PC

  • Gmail: search has:attachment filename:pdf aadhaar (or pan, statement) and delete old emails you don't need, or at least the ones you sent yourself "for safekeeping". older_than:2y narrows it to old mail.
  • Outlook: search hasattachment:yes aadhaar.
  • WhatsApp: delete the media from the chat in WhatsApp itself, on your phone and on the PC. Encrypting a file found in the WhatsApp Desktop folder protects the PC copy (WhatsApp on that PC then shows it as unavailable), but the copy on your phone isn't affected.
  • Cloud drives: if OneDrive or Google Drive syncs these folders, the cloud may also keep older versions. See encrypting before the cloud.

Questions

How do I find Aadhaar card scans on my laptop?

Search File Explorer for "aadhaar OR aadhar OR adhar" from your user folder, and check Downloads, Desktop, Pictures and the WhatsApp Desktop folder. Scans with meaningless names like IMG_2041.jpg won't show up in a name search; Clavis Encrypt's free Find sensitive files reads the text inside pictures and PDFs on your PC and lists likely Aadhaar copies, which you can then encrypt. It can miss blurry photos, so check your usual folders too.

Where are WhatsApp Desktop downloaded documents stored on a PC?

For the WhatsApp app for Windows, files received in chats are kept in a hidden folder: %LOCALAPPDATA%\Packages\5319275A.WhatsAppDesktop_cv1g1gvanyjgm\LocalState\shared\transfers. Paste that into File Explorer's address bar. Files you save with "Save as", or download from WhatsApp Web, go where you chose, usually Downloads.

How do I secure a PAN card copy on my laptop?

Encrypt it. With Clavis Encrypt (free), right-click the file and choose Clavis, then Encrypt, or use Find sensitive files to find and encrypt every PAN copy it spots at once. The file becomes a .enc file with a random name that opens only with your password. Delete extra copies you don't need.

How can I find old bank statements on my computer and protect them?

Search File Explorer for "statement OR passbook" and check Downloads, where net-banking statements usually land. Clavis Encrypt's Find sensitive files also recognises statements by their content (an account statement heading with an IFSC or closing balance), even if the PDF has a generic name, and encrypts the ones you tick.

Is it safe to keep an Aadhaar PDF on my laptop?

It's reasonably safe if the file is encrypted and risky if it isn't: anyone who gets the laptop, including a thief or a repair shop, can open a plain PDF. Encrypt it, use a masked Aadhaar where the full number isn't needed, and delete stray copies in Downloads, email and WhatsApp.

Does Clavis upload my files to check them?

No. Find sensitive files runs only on your PC, using the text recognition built into Windows. Nothing is uploaded, and Clavis doesn't keep a list of what it found. If you choose "Never suggest this file", Clavis keeps only a fingerprint of the file's location, not its name.

Will it find every ID document on my PC?

No tool can promise that. Find sensitive files helps you find documents you may have forgotten, but it can miss blurry photos, documents only in Hindi or Tamil, files with unusual names, and password-protected PDFs whose names don't give them away. You review the list before anything is encrypted.

Is Find sensitive files free?

Yes. Find sensitive files, including encrypting everything it finds at once, is part of Clavis Encrypt Free. Paid editions add folders, automation and cloud-folder features.
