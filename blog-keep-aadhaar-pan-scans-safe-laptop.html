Is it safe to keep Aadhaar and PAN scans on your laptop? How to protect them

Almost everyone in India has a folder like this: Aadhaar front and back, PAN card, passport, a cancelled cheque, maybe a bank statement, saved for the next KYC form. It's convenient. It's also a ready-made identity kit for anyone who gets hold of your laptop, phone or email. Here's how to keep those scans and still have them handy.

Short answer: yes, if they're encrypted. Plain scans in a "Documents" or "KYC" folder can be read by anyone who gets your laptop: a thief, a repair shop, or someone who borrows it. Encrypt the files with a free tool such as Clavis Encrypt (AES-256-GCM, works offline), use a masked Aadhaar wherever the full number isn't needed, and don't leave ID copies in your email, WhatsApp or Downloads folder.

The real risks of unprotected ID scans

  • Lost or stolen laptops and phones: a Windows or phone lock screen doesn't encrypt a file someone can copy off the drive.
  • Repair shops and service centres often ask for your password and have full access while the device is with them.
  • Shared family PCs: everyone with access can open the folder. See keeping files private on a shared PC.
  • Old copies everywhere: email attachments, WhatsApp media, Downloads and cloud backups quietly keep copies for years.
  • Misuse: ID copies can be used for fraudulent loan applications, SIM cards or accounts opened in your name.

How to protect your ID documents (step by step)

  1. Collect them in one folder. Search your PC, Downloads, email and WhatsApp Desktop for "aadhaar", "pan" and "passport", and move or delete the stray copies.
  2. Install Clavis Encrypt (free; Windows 10, Windows 11 and Linux), create a vault with a strong password, and keep the one-time recovery key safe.
  3. Encrypt each scan: right-click → Clavis → Encrypt. The file becomes a .enc file with a random name, so even the name "Aadhaar" isn't visible.
  4. When a form asks for a copy, double-click the encrypted file to open it, upload it, and close it. It stays encrypted on your disk.
  5. Download a masked Aadhaar from the official site myaadhaar.uidai.gov.in (choose "Do you want a masked Aadhaar?"). It shows only the last four digits, and is accepted for many purposes.
  6. Before sending a copy by email, encrypt it. See how to encrypt files before emailing. Or password-protect the PDF.

Masked Aadhaar: what it is and when to use it

A masked Aadhaar replaces the first eight digits of your Aadhaar number with "xxxx-xxxx", showing only the last four. In May 2022 UIDAI advised people to share masked copies instead of photocopies; the advisory was withdrawn two days later because it could be misinterpreted, and the official position is now to use "normal prudence" when sharing Aadhaar. A masked copy remains the safer choice wherever an organisation doesn't need your full number.

Questions

Is it safe to keep my Aadhaar card scan on my laptop?

It's safe if the file is encrypted. A plain scan can be read by anyone who gets the laptop, including thieves and repair shops. Encrypt it with a file-encryption app such as Clavis Encrypt, and use a masked Aadhaar where the full number isn't needed.

What is a masked Aadhaar and where do I download it?

A masked Aadhaar shows only the last four digits of your Aadhaar number. Download it from the official UIDAI site, myaadhaar.uidai.gov.in, by choosing the masked option when downloading your e-Aadhaar.

Should I share my Aadhaar photocopy on WhatsApp or email?

Avoid it where you can. Copies stay in chats, email and backups for years and are easy to forward. Share a masked Aadhaar if the full number isn't required, and encrypt or password-protect files you send.

How do I password-protect my PAN card PDF?

Encrypt the file with Clavis Encrypt (right-click → Clavis → Encrypt), or use a PDF tool that adds a password. Encryption hides the file's name too, so it isn't obvious what the file is.
