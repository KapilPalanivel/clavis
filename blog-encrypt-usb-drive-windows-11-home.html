How to encrypt a USB drive on Windows 11 Home (without BitLocker)

A USB stick is the easiest thing in the world to lose: it falls out of a bag, gets left in a printer, or is borrowed and never returned. Whatever is on it — tax papers, ID scans, a backup of your photos — goes with it. Encrypting the stick means a finder sees nothing but locked files.

On Windows 11 Pro you'd use BitLocker To Go. On Windows 11 Home you can't turn it on. Here are the ways that do work on Home, and how to pick one.

Option 1: BitLocker To Go, set up on a Pro PC

If you have access to a PC running Windows 11 Pro, plug the stick in there, right-click it in File Explorer and choose Turn on BitLocker. Windows 11 Home can then unlock and use that stick with its password; it just can't encrypt a new one.

  • Good: the whole stick is encrypted, and it opens on any Windows PC with the password.
  • Catch: you need a Pro PC to create it, macOS and Linux can't open it without extra tools, and once unlocked, everything on it is readable.

Option 2: a password-protected 7-Zip archive

With the free 7-Zip, select your files → 7-Zip → Add to archive, choose 7z, set a password, tick Encrypt file names, and save the archive to the stick.

  • Good: free, works on every edition of Windows, and opens anywhere 7-Zip is installed.
  • Catch: to change one file you extract, edit and re-pack the whole archive, and the extracted copies tend to be forgotten on whichever PC you used. A lost password can't be recovered.

Option 3: VeraCrypt

VeraCrypt can encrypt the whole stick or keep an encrypted container file on it, which you mount as a drive letter.

  • Good: strong, free and open source, and works across Windows, macOS and Linux.
  • Catch: every PC where you open the stick needs VeraCrypt, and installing it needs administrator rights — so work or school PCs are often out. Mounting and unmounting adds steps, and a lost password can't be recovered.

Option 4: encrypt the files on the stick with Clavis

Clavis encrypts files where they are, so you can lock the files on your USB stick directly — no container, no archive, no Pro edition of Windows. It installs without administrator rights.

  1. Install Clavis and create your vault. Write down or print the recovery key it shows you.
  2. Plug in the stick and open it in File Explorer.
  3. Right-click a file → Show more options → Clavis → Encrypt. Or drag files from the stick onto the Clavis window and press Encrypt now.

Each file becomes a .enc file with a random name, so even the file names give nothing away. Clavis Free encrypts files one at a time; Clavis Pro encrypts a whole folder on the stick in one go.

To open the files on another PC, that PC needs Clavis and your vault. Our guide to moving encrypted files to a new PC shows how to bring your vault along.

Whatever you choose, keep a second copy. USB sticks fail without warning, and encryption doesn't change that. Keep the originals — or an encrypted backup — somewhere other than the stick, and keep your password or recovery key somewhere other than the stick too.

Questions

Can Windows 11 Home encrypt a USB drive with BitLocker?

No. Windows 11 Home can unlock and use a USB stick that was encrypted with BitLocker To Go on a Pro PC, but it can't turn BitLocker on for a new one. Use 7-Zip, VeraCrypt or a file-encryption app such as Clavis instead.

How do I password-protect a USB stick for free?

Put the files in a password-protected 7-Zip archive (7z format, Encrypt file names ticked), create a VeraCrypt container on the stick, or encrypt the files with Clavis Free. All three are free.

Will the encrypted files open on someone else's computer?

Only if that computer has the right tool and you have the password: 7-Zip for an archive, VeraCrypt for a container, Clavis and your vault for .enc files, and any Windows PC for BitLocker To Go.

Is it safe to keep my only copy on an encrypted USB stick?

No. Encryption protects against people reading the files, not against the stick breaking or getting lost. Always keep a second copy somewhere else.
